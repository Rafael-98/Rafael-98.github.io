Local constraints in algebraic classical spin liquids give rise to singular momentum-space correlations, the so-called pinch points. Each pinch point carries a momentum-space winding number that determines the low-energy structure of the spin liquid. We show that nonmagnetic vacancies in two dimensions reveal this topological invariant in the real-space winding of the surrounding spin textures. We prove this bulk-defect correspondence for rank-$$n$$ U(1) spin liquids under explicit conditions and verify it numerically for the Coulomb spin liquids of the kagome and checkerboard lattices and for the rank-2 U(1) spin liquid of the honeycomb-snowflake model. Vacancies can therefore serve as general probes for distinguishing the emergent gauge theories of classical disordered magnets.
