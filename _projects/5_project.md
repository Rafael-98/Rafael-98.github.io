We investigate the spin-1/2 XXZ model on the triangular lattice with strong Ising anisotropy, motivated by recent experiments. DMRG simulations of the dynamical spin structure factor agree closely with inelastic neutron scattering data on the layered compound K$$_2$$Co(SeO$$_3$$)$$_2$$. The low-energy spectrum shows a roton-like minimum at the $$M$$ point, absent in linear spin-wave theory, together with a broad continuum above it and an approximately linear dispersion with vanishing spectral weight near $$\Gamma$$. Hard-core boson approaches (including self-consistent Schwinger bosons) and a variational supersolid quantum-dimer ansatz each reproduce parts of these features. The supersolid QDM wavefunction and the DMRG ground state have nearly identical structure factors with pronounced transverse photon-like excitations, which explains the microscopic origin of the supersolid excitations and their proximity to the spin liquid phase seen experimentally.
